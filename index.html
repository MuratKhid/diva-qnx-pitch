The Problem

The ocean outlasts every battery.

According to US National Ocean Service, 75% of the seafloor is unmapped. Most underwater vehicles burn majority of their energy fighting drag with propellers. Unlike other underwater vehicles, DIVA uses the manta ray's mechanics, utilizing the wings to make moving nearly free.

Biomechanics

It doesn't swim. It glides.

An internal ballast engine makes DIVA heavier while the hydrofoil hull converts the descent into a forward glide. Purging the water allows it to lift the same way. The result is a sawtooth gliding motion that minimizes energy consumption.

Part two

With the hardware covered, here's how QNX comes into play -
the control stack.

The invisible half

DIVA's pitch angle is its speed, its range, its glide ratio. The loop that holds that angle has to tick on time — every time — while a vision pipeline chews camera frames beside it. That is not a job for a best-effort OS. That is a job for QNX Neutrino.

QNX use nº 1 · Architecture

Resource managers wrap the IMU, depth sensor and camera as clean POSIX devices. Message passing feeds a priority-scheduled control core; actuators get their commands on schedule. Vision hangs off the side — important, never critical.

QNX use nº 2 · Determinism

ΔX/ΔZ = cot θ — jitter in the pitch loop literally shortens the glide. QNX's priority scheduler holds the 100 Hz cadence no matter what inference is doing. On a general-purpose kernel, the same loop drifts.

QNX use nº 3 · Fault isolation

Under a microkernel, the vision pipeline is just a process in its own address space. It can die mid-dive; the process manager restarts it; the flight loop never misses a tick. On a monolithic stack, that same fault is a drowned robot.

the demo we'll run for you: kill the vision process mid-glide in the pool. DIVA keeps flying. Try that on a monolith.

Why QNX is essential

Determinism

Fixed-cadence control

Priority scheduling and priority inheritance keep the pitch loop mathematically honest — glide ratio depends on it.

Isolation

Crashes stay small

User-space drivers and a sandboxed vision pipeline: a bug costs a frame, never the vehicle.
